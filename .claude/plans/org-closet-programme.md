## Where things stand (read this first; 2026-09-16, end of the discussion sessions)

This file is a running record of a multi-day thinking-through, so later sections supersede earlier ones. The current state of every point, in one place:

**What it is.** The Virtual Closet: a page in an LGBTQ+ centre's name where its community stocks a closet of gender-affirming underwear and swimwear, and RUBIES matches every dollar. The centre shares one link. Nothing to maintain, nothing to pay. Language everywhere is **matching**, never discount/wholesale/50%. Community-facing copy says "shipment"; centre-facing copy says "box".

**Three doors.** (1) Buy: 20% off a first order, applied automatically at checkout through a hidden single-use discount drawn per click from the centre's pool; no code is ever shown or typed; for every two pairs bought through the link the closet gets one (25% of the order into the box, matched); a 30-day browser window from the link also credits later orders (credit, not discount); code beats window. (2) Apply for a free pair: name they go by, items (style, colour, size), pickup or ship (ship expands to collect an address, held by RUBIES only), "what would a pair mean for you" (required; the org's basis for manual approval and RUBIES' feedback), opt-in to share words anonymously, email. Approved automatically within limits the centre sets (items per request, requests per person per year); free pairs are final; out-of-stock items are auto-swapped to the closest style in that size. (3) Sponsor: tiles priced per item at half retail ($16 a pair of underwear, $21 a bra, $24 a bikini bottom, $50, $100, $300 a full shipment); no email; matched.

**The box.** One open box per centre, numbered, sequential. Funding target set by the centre, $300 minimum. Goal shown is the larger of the target and the cost of all approved requests (half retail per item + $15 per shipped package), so "everyone who asked gets theirs" is always true. At the goal it ships as double the product ($300 → $600). Past the goal the centre chooses: send now or keep growing (no automatic rollover, no six-month auto-ship). A request made against a funded shipment joins a wait list for the next, in order. Requested items first; the centre fills the rest or lets it auto-fill with popular in-stock items. Box menu for now: AJ, Charlie, Sassy, Brooke bra, Ruby bikini bottom (65-80% margin at the matched price, deepest stock). Shipping to a door: $15 per package charged to the box, internal. Progress shown to the community as dollars raised of goal, GoFundMe style; ledger box shows counts (sponsors, orders, requests, waiting).

**Economics.** Every box dollar is cash to RUBIES; RUBIES delivers $2 retail per dollar. Net per matched item on the menu: $12-19 (65-80%). Buyer order at 20% off nets $12.72 (50%). All mixes positive; worst case all-code-credit boxes ~46%. Losers excluded from the menu: gaff (negative), Stella, basic chest pads. Nitro costs: $7.90 per single package, $15.49 per box. Excluded from figures: payment processing ~3%.

**The org's side.** Sees the request list per box (name, items, words) to hand pairs out; email and address stay with RUBIES. Settings: sizes offered, items per request, requests per year, target, pause requests, send-now when funded. Approval automatic by default, manual by email if preferred.

**Audience rule.** Buyers see one sentence; requesters see timing only; sponsors see a goal in dollars; the centre sees the mechanics in the terms.

**Not adopted / dropped:** per-request "cover it" sponsoring; item-count and product-value units on the page; a RUBIES float per org; the 90-day continued credit; the six-month auto-ship; the email step on the buyer side; "nobody at the closet sees your order"; the physical stand as the first move (parked behind the virtual programme); parallel boxes.

## The programme in one paragraph

An org gets a display in its space with take-home cards carrying a QR code. A community member who scans it gets 15% off at rubyshines.com, and every item they buy puts the same item into the org's next box. Allies can also sponsor the box directly with a round-dollar gift. Everything accrues in dollars on a ledger toward a $300 box. When the box fills, the org chooses whatever product it wants at wholesale prices and RUBIES sends double. Nothing ships until the box fills.

## Where the thinking has landed so far (all still open)

1. **Credit is paid in product, never cash, for orgs.** Cash costs a dollar; product costs COGS ($3.32 for AJ underwear, $4.12 for the Ruby bikini bottom).
2. **Credit rate: 25% of retail price** (not of what the customer paid). With the 1:1 match at box time this is 50% of retail in product, and wholesale is 50% of retail, so a referred item becomes one item in a doubled box. **Customer-facing copy says "25% of your order goes to the closet" (Jamie, 2026-09-14), not one-for-one:** one-for-one is only true for pairs picked up from a doubled box, and false for a pair shipped to a home (no doubling, so a $32 order earns half a pair). 25% is true everywhere and needs no explanation. Jamie pitched 15% to Uniting Pride; 25% is the working number because the marginal cost is COGS-only. The three numbers (credit rate, match, wholesale rate) must move together or one-for-one breaks.
3. **Customer discount: 20% off** (Jamie, 2026-09-14 evening; was 15%).
4. **The unit is dollars everywhere. Pairs are worked examples only.** Referred credit is a percentage, sponsors give round amounts, orgs top up from budgets, and a mixed box never divides into pairs. Converting to pairs anywhere except examples needs a rounding rule.
5. **Sponsorship is a product sale, not a donation, and there is no wallet.** Money paid to a for-profit company is not a donation and a college crowd will ask why. Pooled money creates a held-funds liability and a "what if they never reach $300" problem. Instead: a Shopify product "Sponsor [org]'s closet" in round amounts. Ordinary sale, Shopify handles tax.
6. **Nothing ships per sponsor gift.** The sponsor product is flagged non-shipping in Shopify so it never appears in Warehance as a pick. Shipping cost is per box, not per gift.
7. **A box is $300 at wholesale, matched.** The org chooses the mix; RUBIES sends double of whatever they choose. Same $300 minimum as today's org purchasing.
8. ~~Partial box ships after six months if it stalls.~~ **Removed for now (Jamie, 2026-09-16).** A box ships when it reaches $300; no time-based auto-ship. Revisit if boxes stall in the pilot.
9. **An org leaving the programme gets a final box** of whatever is on the ledger. Sponsors paid for that closet; shipping closes the obligation.
10. **Attribution is the org's discount code**, not a cookie or a 30-day window. Whoever uses the code is theirs, forever. Drop the "within 30 days" language from the pitch.
11. **The QR resolves to a RUBIES-controlled short link**, never a raw Shopify discount URL. Redirects to Shopify's native `/discount/CODE?redirect=...` apply-URL. Gives scan counts, re-pointable printed QRs, and a per-org landing page.
12. **Build in-house.** GoAffPro and Shopify Collabs pay cash on cookie attribution; neither does in-kind credit, matching, or a box goal. The org programme is a ledger, a per-org page, and four email types, all on patterns rubies-automations already has.
13. **Match framing is US-only wording.** The international tier is 30% (duties, shipping), so "we send double" cannot be universal. International orgs keep percent language. Out of scope for now anyway.
14. **Underwear is the unit and the display product.** AJ no-tuck underwear ($28 retail, $14 wholesale) is the everyday item closets are asked for and the starter-run item. The bikini bottom is the story piece on the display, not the unit.
15. **Physical display only where there is a door.** Programme profile (`standing_closet` with foot traffic) decides physical stand vs digital kit. By-request orgs get the digital kit: same code and link, no display, zero logistics.
16. **Naming:** to orgs, "affiliate" sounds like influencer marketing and they are already partners. Fold it into the partnership. Working name "closet programme"; customer-facing name TBD (something like "Pairs for the Closet").

## Economics (US, per referred AJ order)

**Price correction (2026-09-14):** the $28 used below is the KIDS AJ price (sizes 4-10, SKU AJ-PNK-4 in the margin tool). Adult AJ (XS-4X) is **$32** on the live store, so adult wholesale is $16 and every dollar figure below scales by 32/28. Adult store prices used in the prototype: AJ $32, Charlie $33, Sassy $32, Brooke $42, Ava $46, Ruby $48.

| | |
|---|---|
| Cash in after 15% customer discount | $23.80 |
| COGS of the pair they bought | $3.32 |
| COGS of the pair the closet gets (matched credit) | $3.32 |
| Margin | ~72% |

Credit rate comparison (15% was pitched on the call; 25% is the working number):

| Credit rate | Credit on a $28 order | Product after match | COGS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15% | $4.20 | $8.40 (~0.6 pair) | ~$2.00 |
| 25% | $7.00 | $14.00 (1 pair) | ~$3.32 |

**Referred credit alone will not fill boxes.** At a realistic 3-5 orders/month from a lobby QR, credit fills a $300 box in many months. The sponsor drive is the engine; credit is the topper that keeps the org engaged between drives.

**Gaff check (deferred):** Naomi gaff COGS was $21.70 against a $17.50 wholesale price, so matching one loses money. Gaffs are not sold to orgs until the next production order lands; when it does, re-check COGS before allowing gaffs in a matched box. If still upside down, exclude gaffs from the match.

**Cannibalisation:** low. The lobby scanner is new-to-RUBIES or was sent by the org; the incremental discount over the first-time offer is 5 points. Orgs like Uniting Pride ($1,500/yr, mostly binders) were not buying RUBIES anyway, so sponsor/credit product is net new, not displaced wholesale.

**Code leakage:** a per-org code posted to a coupon site earns the org credit on random internet orders. Acceptable at this scale (credit is COGS-cheap); monitor per-code order counts and geography.

**Accounting note:** sponsor money is collected weeks before the product ships, so it is deferred revenue until the box goes out. Same shape as pre-orders. One line to the accountant.

### 2. The display and the kit
- **The take-home card is the real display.** People scan in a lobby but buy at home, so the card carries the QR and the code. A small stand with ~100 tear-off cards.
- **The starter size run is the fitting kit.** Orgs like Uniting Pride already fit people for binders in person. The stand holds the AJ run in black (full size range), a measuring tape, and a size-finder QR.
- **Three lines on the card:** try it here; order online with 15% off; every pair you buy sends a pair to this closet. Fine print: "toward the closet's next box".
- **Who the card speaks to (2026-09-13):** the lobby holds wearers (the majority; teens to fifties), parents buying for a kid, and allies/staff who will never wear it. The take-home card is written to the wearer only (one headline; two asks on a business card dilute both, same rule as emails); "you" still reads correctly for a parent. The **one-for-one line is the bridge** to supporters: it says the closet gets product without asking the ally for anything yet. The **landing page carries the fork**, two buttons up top: "Shop for me" (applies the code, goes to sizing) and "Send a pair to the closet" (sponsor amounts). The **tent card carries both lines** because it is read standing still: wearer headline, smaller line "Not shopping for yourself? Send a pair to the closet." **Never label the reader:** "underwear for trans girls and women" tells a parent or ally the card is not for them and can identify a wearer in a public lobby; "no-tuck underwear and swimwear" says what it is without saying who is holding it.
- **Lobby, not closet: the audience is mostly non-wearers (2026-09-13).** The display sits in the org's lobby, so most people passing it will never wear the product. Consequences: (1) **The tent card speaks to the community first**: this centre's closet gives out free gender-affirming underwear, every pair bought here sends one to the closet, help fill the box; the wearer line is second and discreet ("Need a pair? Ask at the desk, or scan"), so a wearer is never addressed in front of the room. (2) **Cards are a staff hand-out, not a counter dispenser**: a wearer may not want to scan a sign in public; the private path is a card handed over at the desk, in the closet, or at a fitting, the same moment staff hand out binders, and a parent can carry one home. The staff card says "hand these out". (3) **The digital kit is the real private take-home** for by-request orgs: the link and code go in the org's gear request form, newsletter, Discord; nobody touches paper. One message per piece: tent card = community + sponsor, then wearer; staff card = wearer only with the code; digital kit = same link in private channels; landing page = the fork. The garment on the stand stays in the lobby; it is what makes the sponsor ask concrete.
- **Org name:** the physical piece says "this closet / this centre"; the landing page carries the org's name and logo from the registry (logos already on Shopify CDN).
- **Logistics split:** stand + starter run ship from Warehance as a $0 order tagged like samples (existing pattern, `samples_shipped_at`). Cards ship direct from the printer to the org. The warehouse never handles personalisation.
- **Staff card under the base.** Written for whoever inherits the stand: what this is, what the closet earns, how to get more cards, QR to the org's own progress page. A new coordinator in September should adopt it in two minutes without RUBIES in the loop. This is why physical matters: the stand outlives the person who agreed to it.
- **Digital kit** for every partner regardless of display: link, QR PNG, an Instagram tile, an email blurb, a line for their intake form.

### 3. Attribution and the landing page
- QR → `rubyshines.com/r/<slug>` (or a Railway endpoint) → logs the scan → 302 to Shopify `/discount/<CODE>?redirect=/pages/partners/<slug>`.
- Landing page per org, built on the **published-JSON pattern** (same as donation partners and store locator): org name + logo, the 15% code, a size finder, **email capture** so the code follows them home (Klaviyo profile tagged with the org; gives leads per org even when they do not buy), the progress bar, and the sponsor button.
- Sponsor button → `/cart/add?id=<variant>&properties[org]=<slug>`; the line-item property is the attribution.
- **"New here?" path** on the page: one tap to say "I'm the new contact", lands in the outreach book as a contact change. Turnover becomes an inbound event instead of a bounce discovered months later.
- **Cart add-on** on rubyshines.com for referred customers: "add $X for [org]'s closet". The person who just got 15% off is the most natural sponsor. Non-shipping, goes to the ledger.
- Shopify: one discount code per order; set `combines_with` deliberately against automatic discounts and Simple Bundles. Build detail.

#### Attribution: three layers, no app (2026-09-13)
Code-only misses people who scan in a lobby and buy weeks later from a search without the code; the org loses credit and the customer loses 15%. Three layers, strongest first:
1. **The code on the order.** Explicit, cross-device, no expiry.
2. **The email.** Landing page asks for an email to send the code; the Klaviyo profile is tagged with the org, so any later order from that address attributes on any device, code or not. This is what cookie programmes cannot do, and it is the layer that matters most here (scan on a phone, buy on a laptop at home is the normal path).
3. **The browser.** Short link lands with a ref param; theme code stores it (cookie + localStorage) and writes it into cart attributes at checkout so it lands on the order. **Twist: if a stored ref is present and no code applied, the theme applies the org's code automatically**, so the customer who forgot still gets 15%. Limits: Safari caps script-set cookies at 7 days (localStorage does the work there, and degrades); per device. Layer 2 exists because of both.

Rules: windows are code forever, email and browser 90 days (considered purchase, people save up). Conflicts (rare): code beats email beats browser. Repeat orders after the window earn nothing unless the code is used again, which is why the code stays printed on the take-home card.

### 4. The ledger and the box
- Ledger table, entries in dollars: referred-order credit (25% of retail), sponsor gift, org top-up, box shipment debit. Nightly job over the orders mirror keyed on discount code + line-item property. Idempotent on order id.
- Goal = $300. Progress page shows dollars and worked examples: "$300 fills a box. That's 40 pairs of underwear, or 26 bikini bottoms, or your own mix."
- **Box-ready flow (v1 = today's manual flow):** org replies to "your box is ready" with styles and sizes in plain language; existing `parse_wholesale_input` turns it into lines; operator creates a $0 wholesale order via `create_wholesale_order` with quantities doubled; ledger debits.
- Rules: partial box after six months; final box on exit; gaff check when gaffs return.

### 5. Notifications and engagement

- Sponsor thank-you with the org's name (Shopify order confirmation customised, or a Klaviyo flow).
- **The org's own progress page is the engagement lever**: they share it to their community themselves ("help us fill the box"). No public leaderboard; orgs do not like being ranked.
- **Refills are the natural touchpoint.** Cards run out and boxes ship once or twice a year, so the semester rhythm is built in rather than bolted on.
- Cadence guard: monthly when active, quarterly when idle. Do not over-email orgs.

### 6. Dashboard
- A section in the Outreach detail pane (or a panel tab): balance, scans, orders, box status, last statement sent.

## Direction under exploration: the virtual closet (2026-09-14, nothing decided)

Jamie's idea: a white-labelled "virtual closet" per org, listing the items the closet holds (RUBIES and non-RUBIES, e.g. binders), with fundraising and purchasing integrated through RUBIES; possibly a partner brand for trans-masc gear.

**Why it pulls the programme together:** the landing page, progress bar, sponsor page, digital kit and the org's own request form collapse into one thing the org actually wants: a public page for its closet saying what it has, what it needs, and how to help. Consequences:
- **Sponsor-an-item replaces "give $25".** A registry ("the closet needs four pairs in size M, sponsor one") converts better than a donation button because the giver sees exactly what they did. Feeds the same dollar ledger toward the box.
- **Demand signal by size.** Fixes the random-sizes problem Jamie raised on the Uniting Pride call: the matched box is filled with what the org is short of, not a guessed mix.

## Economics with the pooled model (2026-09-14, evening)

Inputs (real): AJ adult $32 retail, COGS $3.32; single-package fulfilment $7.90; box shipment $15.49 (~19 items); pool charged $15 per package shipped to a door; buyer credit 25% of retail; **customer discount now 20%** (Jamie, was 15%); RUBIES matches every pool dollar (each pool dollar buys $2 of retail product).

## Centre settings, dynamic goal, wait list, request fields (Jamie, 2026-09-16, built)

- **Centre settings:** items per request, how often one person may ask (per year), the sizes offered, and the **funding target with a $300 floor** (the minimum shipment). All on the survey and in the centre's view; on the prototype, in the presenter panel and as link parameters (`items`, `peryear`, `target`).
- **Dynamic goal:** the goal shown is the larger of the centre's target and the cost of every approved request (each item at half retail, plus $15 if shipped). So "everyone who asked gets theirs" stays true, and sponsors see "$96 more covers them". When requests exceed the target there is no stock remainder; the shipment is all requests.
- **Wait list:** a request made while the open shipment is already funded joins the list for the next one, strictly in order of approval; the requester is told how many are ahead. The centre can pause new requests if the list runs ahead of funding.
- **Sequential shipments:** exactly one open box per centre, numbered; when the centre sends it, the next opens with the wait list already in it and its goal reflecting their cost. No parallel boxes; a campaign name and date on the current box if a centre wants a drive. History shown as shipments sent.
- **Send it now** is a centre action once the $300 minimum is met (prototype: presenter "Send the shipment").
- **Request fields:** the name they go by (never a legal name), items (style, colour, size), pickup or ship, "What would a pair mean for you?" (one or two sentences, required, the free-swimwear pattern: the org's basis for a manual approval and RUBIES' feedback loop), email, and an opt-in checkbox "You may share my words, without my name, to encourage sponsors" (default off; only ticked stories are ever shown, always anonymous). The org sees name, items and words to hand pairs over; email and address stay with RUBIES; identity is dropped after a fixed period once the shipment is done.
